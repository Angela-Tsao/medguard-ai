## Recommended — the 3 numbers for deck page 2 (30 seconds)

Use these three, in this order. They are US, older-adult
specific, and each maps to the demo (warfarin + NSAID is
exactly the bleeding combination in number 3).

1. **43% of US adults 65+ take 5 or more prescription
   medications** — CDC, Health, United States
   (2017–March 2020; up from 33.3% in 2001–2004).
   Polypharmacy is usually defined as 5+ medications.
2. **About 99,600 emergency hospitalizations a year for
   adverse drug events in adults 65+** — Budnitz et al.,
   NEJM 2011. Nearly half were in people 80+, and four drug
   groups were involved in 67% of them: warfarin 33%,
   insulins 14%, oral antiplatelet drugs (e.g. clopidogrel)
   13%, oral diabetes medications 11%.
3. **Adverse drug reactions / events cause up to 30% of
   hospital admissions in older adults, at about $30 billion
   a year in the US** — systematic review, Age & Ageing
   2025 (54(8):afaf231; 9 studies, 2015–2025). A single ADR
   costs €2,250–€9,538 per event, and an ADR admission can
   prolong a hospital stay by up to 12 days.

## Supporting numbers (use only if asked in Q&A)

- **ED visits:** about 4 in every 1,000 people in the US
  visit an emergency department each year because of an
  adverse drug event; adults 65+ made up 34.5% of those
  visits, and 43.6% of older adults who went to the ED for
  a drug reaction were admitted — the highest rate of any
  age group. Shehab et al., JAMA 2016.
- **Cost per 1,000 older adults:** annual ADE-related costs
  approach $65,000 per 1,000 older ambulatory adults, with
  more than a third associated with preventable events.
  Field et al., Med Care 2005;43(12):1171–1176 (via AHRQ
  PSNet).
- **What causes the admissions:** in the 2025 systematic
  review, the most frequent ADR/ADEs causing hospitalisation
  were falls (19.4–20.9%), delirium (7.3–12.9%) and
  bleeding (8–30.2%). ADR prevalence as a cause of
  hospitalisation ranged 3.3–23.1%; ADE 11.75–18%.
- **Bleeding drug interactions cost more:** in an Irish
  cohort of 782 older adults acutely admitted to hospital,
  a drug–drug interaction that increases bleeding risk
  raised the odds of an ADR-related admission (aOR 1.83)
  and added a median €3,520 to the admission cost.
  Int J Clin Pharm. 2025;47:1239–1247. (Ireland, not US —
  say so if you cite it; it is the closest direct
  warfarin+NSAID-type cost figure found.)

## Do NOT use in the deck

- **Lown Institute "$62 billion over a decade"** (cutting
  unnecessary medications could save up to $62bn in
  avoidable hospital stays) and **"750 older adults
  hospitalized every day / 5 million sought care in 2018 /
  150,000 premature deaths by 2030"** — these are Lown
  Institute estimates reported second-hand (via AARP /
  Substack summaries), not peer-reviewed primary studies.
  If a reviewer asks, you may mention them only as
  "Lown Institute estimates", never as established fact.
- **UK figures** (£2.21bn/year NHS over-prescribing cost;
  1,000 elderly admissions a day in England) — UK system,
  not US; the deck's user is US-based.
- **$4,700 per admission / $2bn a year (US hospitals,
  2000)** — from the Institute of Medicine "To Err Is
  Human" era; superseded by the 2025 review above.
